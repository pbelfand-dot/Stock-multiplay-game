# What a real co-driver does, and how it becomes gameplay

You said the co-driver can look out of the car and should get the jobs a real co-driver has. Here's
what real co-drivers actually do (checked), and the in-game task each one turns into.

## The real job (checked)

| Real job | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Read the pacenotes** | The main job. Pacenotes describe the road (corners, distances, crests, junctions) in shorthand, read out loud to the driver at speed |
| **Recce** | Before the rally the crew drives each stage slowly in a normal car, and the co-driver **writes the notes**. Some events ban recce and hand out official notes instead |
| **Time card and time controls** | The time card is the co-driver's most important document. Between stages there are time controls: **early costs 1 minute per minute early, late costs 10 seconds per minute late**. The time is only entered when both crew members and the car are in the control area |
| **Road book** | Between stages (road sections) the co-driver navigates with a road book: pages of **"tulip" diagrams** (little drawings of each junction) plus distances |
| **Safety after a crash** | Every car carries a **green OK sign** and a **red cross / SOS sign**. After stopping on a stage the crew shows OK (or the red cross if someone is hurt) to the cars behind, and places a **red warning triangle at least 50 m before the car** |
| **Keep the driver calm** | Trust between driver and co-driver matters as much as the notes. (The TikTok meme says it too: *good callouts, always chill, early callouts*) |

## In-game tasks

### First version (phases 1–2)

| Task | How it plays | Why it's fun |
|---|---|---|
| **Call the road** | Look at the map, work out what's coming, yell it. Voice chat, or later a **quick-call wheel** (the AI voice says "LEFT 3!" for you) | The core loop |
| **Wipers + washer** | A puddle covers the **whole** windshield. The co-driver works the wipers (the driver's hands are busy); each stroke clears the arcs more, the corners stay dirty | Straight from the videos: "WIPER! WIPER!" |
| **Start countdown** | Only the co-driver can see the start clock. They count the driver in: "5, 4, 3, 2, 1, GO". Jump the start = +10 s | A tense start every stage |
| **OK board + triangle** | After going off, hold up OK (or the clock takes a penalty). If the car's stuck: get out, **run 50 m back** and place the triangle, then both **push the car** back onto the road | Physical comedy, very clippable |

### Later (phases 4+)

| Task | How it plays |
|---|---|
| **Time card between stages** | Drive the road section to the next stage and arrive **on the exact minute**. Too early hurts more than late (like the real rules) |
| **Road book navigation** | Tulip diagrams for the junctions and villages between stages. Wrong turn = lost time |
| **Recce mode** | Drive the stage slowly first. The co-driver **writes their own notes** on the map device, then has to live with them on the real run |
| **Punctures** | Both get out, jack the car up, swap the wheel together, against the clock |
| **Night and fog stages** | The co-driver handles lights, fog lights and the demister |
| **Spectators / animals** | A cow on the road, fans standing too close: spot them out the window and call them |

## Our map device (not their tablet)

*Left Left Left!* uses an orange GPS-style tablet. Ours is a **wooden map board** with brass clips
and a stopwatch clipped on top.

It shows **only the map and where the car is**: the road ahead as a line, heading-up like a GPS.
No grades, no "4 LEFT", no warnings. Looking at a bend and turning it into a call fast enough is the
whole skill of the seat, and that's what makes it fun. (The AI co-driver, for solo, does use
generated pacenotes.)

## One thing the game can't do

**The game can't hear voice chat.** It doesn't know if the co-driver yelled "left" at the right
moment, so it can't grade their calls directly.

So we score the **team** instead: stage time, times off the road, crashes, penalties. Calls made
with the quick-call wheel *can* be graded for timing (early = good). Speech recognition could come
later, but it's not needed.
